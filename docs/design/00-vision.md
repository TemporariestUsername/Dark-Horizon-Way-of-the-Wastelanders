# 00 · Vision

## The pitch
> *"In Apocalypse World the horizons are dark, and no roads go to them."* (The Driver)

**Dark Horizon: Way of the Wastelanders** is a single‑player, open‑world exploration, trade and survival RPG set in **Apocalypse World**.

For a generation, the world's psychic maelstrom **howled** on every open road. Anyone who went more than a day out from a hold heard it, and either didn't come back or came back wrong. So everyone stayed home. The great interstates of the golden age lay empty for twenty‑five years.

Then, a little over a year ago, **it eased**.

Now the roads are quiet enough to drive, and nobody alive knows what's out there. You take your rig and your crew down **the Ten**, up **the Fifty‑Five**, across **the Forty**: the interstates are this world's oceans, and you sail them the way Koei's captains sailed theirs.
- You chart roads nobody has driven since the Howling.
- You make first contact with holds that have been strangers for a generation.
- You buy where there's plenty and sell where there's want.
- You put your jingle into the holds that matter until they lean your way.
- You push toward the edges, where the maelstrom still howls.

Meanwhile, six powerful hardholders who were only rumors last year are racing to win the newly reachable holds: the Lighthouse on the Memphis bridge, Sweetwell on its aquifer, Highwater on the Lakes, the Exchange in its Florida vault, Burning Water in its refineries, and Cerro in its mountain walls. Underneath them, cults, road gangs, guilds and secret societies pick sides. And in the dead skyline of Harrowgate, a prophet called **Last** and a choir of brainers say they're the reason the world went quiet. They say they're holding the maelstrom in. People call the sound coming from Harrowgate **the Hum**. It spreads slowly, hold by hold, down the same roads you drive. Nobody's sure it can be stopped, and nobody's sure what happens if it isn't.

## Design thesis: three games become one

| From | We take | Expressed through |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncharted Waters: New Horizons** | **An age of discovery.** Freedom to choose your career. Range as a resource. Trade as the engine. Investment as a way to conquer. Several protagonists sharing one world. | Interstates as seas, the Easing, convoys, supplies, the economy, allegiance, discoveries, 6 playbook campaigns |
| **Inindo: Way of the Ninja** | A living political map that changes month by month whatever you do. NPCs with their own lives. Power you learn by *going somewhere*. Classes, levels, HP, training and grid battles. A tyrant whose shadow keeps growing. | The world turn, faction AI, wandering NPCs, skirmishes and gang wars, maelstrom sites, Last and the Hum |
| **Apocalypse World** | Its **classes** (the playbooks), its **setting** and its **ambiance**: the fiction, the voice, the people, the threats, the maelstrom. Not its rules. | Every class, every place, every line of text |

**The split.** The *mechanics* come from UWNH and Inindo: attributes and levels, skills, fame, crew morale and loyalty, formula battles, shares of influence, events. **Apocalypse World** gives the *who, where and how it feels*. The *look* is full‑colour pixel art inspired by the Mad Max films ([14](14-art-audio-ui.md)).

**The test for every feature:** *does it make Apocalypse World seem real, make the characters' lives not boring, and let us play to find out what happens?* That's AW's MC agenda, kept here as our design agenda, not as a rule system.

## Pillars
1. **The road is the sea, and it's unexplored.**
   - The Easing is the first time in a generation anyone can travel. Every interstate is a voyage into the unknown, and every hold you reach might be a first contact.
   - Living rivers and broken overpasses shape the map the way coastlines shaped UWNH's: crossings are straits, and whoever holds them matters.
2. **Scarcity is the map.**
   - Every road is a bet of water, guzzoline, chow and ammo against distance.
   - Every hold is a list of what it has too much of and what it's dying for.
   - Trade isn't arbitrage. It's *keeping people alive at a profit*.
3. **The world doesn't wait.**
   - Each month factions push their ambitions, threats move, the Choir creeps a little further, and NPCs pursue what they want.
   - You're one important person in a world that already had its own plans.
4. **Numbers you can read.**
   - The rules are UWNH's and Inindo's: attributes, levels, HP, morale, prices and shares.
   - Every check shows its chance before you commit. Every price shows where it came from.
   - Failure costs something, but it's never a dead end.
5. **People are the resource that matters.**
   - Crew, hardholders, rivals and lovers all have names, wants, wages and loyalty.
   - The event system is hard on you, never pointless ([05](05-events.md#antifrustration-rules)).
6. **Dark horizons.**
   - Past the edges of the Quiet the maelstrom still howls (**the Loud**). The Easing is a window, and the Choir's slow creep is the shadow across it. Fog of war, the Loud at the edge of the map, the In inside your head.
   - Some answers about the Golden Age are never given. *Nobody remembers how or why.*

## Player fantasy, by playbook (the six launch careers)
- **Driver**: *I'll drive every road the Howling closed, and then the ones that go into the Loud.* Explorer.
- **Chopper**: *Every road out here belongs to my pack.* Raider and gang boss.
- **Hardholder**: *My caravans feed half the Quiet and they all know it.* Merchant prince with a home to defend.
- **Hocus**: *They follow me because I hear what the maelstrom is saying.* Cult leader and Last's rival prophet.
- **Savvyhead**: *Give me a workspace and a dead machine and I'll make it sing louder than the Hum.* Salvager and builder.
- **Quarantine**: *I was there before. I know what Last is. I'm the only one who does.* The main‑quest spine.

## Tone and content policy
- **Mature.** Profanity is native to the voice. Violence is brutal, and its consequences are serious and lasting.
- **Sex happens off‑screen but counts.** Bonds and affection carry numbers (loyalty, a class flavour bonus, a committed‑partner milestone; see [11](11-crew-and-bonds.md#bonds-and-intimacy-offscreen)), resolved at a fade‑to‑black cut. There's always consent and always an opt‑out: an "Intimacy" toggle hides even the cut‑scene text and keeps the numbers. Intimacy never results from mind‑control or coercion. Those abilities have non‑sexual outcomes only.
- **No sexual content involving minors, ever.** Every intimacy‑capable NPC is explicitly an adult.
- **Slavery, cults and cannibalism** appear *as threats*: shown as horrors, never as mechanics that reward the player for doing them.
- **The Golden Age is never explained.** Hints, yes. Lore dumps, no.

## Non-goals
- **Single‑player only.** No co‑op or multiplayer. AW's group play is carried by the crew, their loyalty and their stories.
- **Windows and macOS only.** No console or mobile target.
- Not a shooter or action game. Combat is tactical, turn‑based and formula‑driven, like the source games.
- Not a survival‑crafting sandbox. There's salvage and repair, but no base‑building beyond the playbook workspaces and holdings.
- Not procedural slop. The map is authored, the holds are authored, and the event system sequences authored threat content.
