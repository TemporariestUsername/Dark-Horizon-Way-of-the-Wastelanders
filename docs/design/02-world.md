# 02 · The World: the Basin

## The premise
Nobody remembers how or why. The oldest people alive remember being children when the cities burned, and they remember the water *leaving*: a long tide that went out and never came back. Where the sea was, there's **the Basin**. It's a bowl of cracked salt, dunes and ship bones about 200 miles across, ringed by the dead cliffs of the old coast.

The port cities of the golden age sit on those cliffs, looking down at nothing. Their survivors still call them **holds**, and still call the salt "the sea". Convoys cross it the way ships did. There are still **lighthouses**, and people still keep them lit, though nobody can say exactly why.

> **Design note.** The drained sea lets UWNH's whole vocabulary of ports, crossings, coastlines, storms, wrecks and the unknown far shore survive *literally* inside Apocalypse World. It costs no lore and adds a mystery: *where did the sea go?*

## The threat map *is* the world map
AW's threat map uses rings (the PCs and their resources, closer, farther, notional) and eight directions. The overworld uses the same layout, so the map the Director reasons with and the map the player looks at are one object.

```
                         NORTH: The Rust Coast
                 UP: The Skyline              IN: The Maelstrom
                  (Harrowgate)                (open your brain)
        WEST:          ┌───────────────────────┐          EAST:
     The Rag‑Wastes    │   FARTHER: old coast  │       The Glass
                       │  ┌─────────────────┐  │
                       │  │ CLOSER: the salt│  │
                       │  │  ┌───────────┐  │  │
                       │  │  │  CENTRE:  │  │  │
                       │  │  │ Lighthouse│  │  │
                       │  │  └───────────┘  │  │
                       │  └─────────────────┘  │
                       └───────────────────────┘
                 DOWN: The Deep Lines         OUT: The Drop
                       SOUTH: The Burn‑Flat   (where the sea went)
```

**Rings** (geographic bands, from the Lighthouse outward):

| Ring | What it is | Threat density | Fog |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centre** | The Shallows around the Lighthouse. Your base of operations. | Low | Always known |
| **Closer** | The inner salt: crossing routes and wreck fields | Medium | Revealed by travel or bought maps |
| **Farther** | The old coastline holds, warlord seats, Up and Down entrances | High | Revealed by travel, rumors, the News |
| **Notional** | The Out (the Drop), the deep In, rumored places | Extreme | Not on the map until a rumor, sight or augury puts it there |

**Octants:**
- **N/S/E/W** are geographic regions.
- **Up and Down** are *vertical* layers you reach through specific entrances: the Skyline's tower lifts, and the Deep Lines' subway mouths.
- **Out** is the far edge, the Driver's frontier.
- **In** is a parallel psychic overworld laid over the whole map, entered by *opening your brain*. See [08](08-travel-and-vehicles.md#the-in).

## Regions

### Centre: The Shallows
The flattest, safest salt, around a lighthouse that once stood on an island. The Shallows have warm brine‑springs, wreck salvage and neutral ground. Every career starts within a few days of here.

### North: The Rust Coast
The cold cliffs of the old northern shore, stacked with dead cranes, container walls and fog. Salvage‑rich and water‑rich, but short on food. **Tum Tum** runs the slave market at Pellet. The weather is fog banks and wind.

### West: The Rag‑Wastes
Dunes, hulks half‑buried in sand, nomad camps. This is chopper country. The **Alpha wolf Dremmer** and his pack, the Teeth, claim every road. The weather is sandstorms.

### East: The Glass
A plain where something burned so hot the sand turned to glass. Under it lie Golden‑Age vaults. **Rothschild the Collector** owns the Exchange, the Basin's only lender. The ground there is a mirror: navigation is bad and heat is lethal at noon.

### South: The Burn‑Flat
The lowest, hottest floor of the Basin, with oil seeps and the eternal fire of **Burning Water**. **Grome the Dictator** controls the Sweetwell, the deepest working well, so Grome controls water. The heat is lethal and the water costs are high.

### Up: The Skyline
The broken towers of **Harrowgate**, a golden‑age city on the eastern headland: antenna farms, sky‑bridges, and a crashed airship hanging between two towers. **Last** and the Choir live here. You reach it from the ground through three lift‑towers.

### Down: The Deep Lines
Subways, storm drains, bunkers and the old desalination pipes that once fed the cities. **Mother Krin**, a Hive queen, runs it with her Family. It connects regions underground, so it's a shortcut if you dare. It's pitch dark and has no fuel use, because you walk.

### Out: The Drop
At the Basin's far edge the floor tilts down into a trench no one has crossed, where the dust‑wind pours *downward*. Rumor says there's water at the bottom, or the sea, or the dead. It's the Driver's destination and the last region of the game.

### In: The Maelstrom
It isn't a place, but it has places: sites where the world's psychic maelstrom runs thin or thick. See [08](08-travel-and-vehicles.md#the-in).

## Last and the Hum (the campaign antagonist)
**What everyone knows.** Months ago, every radio in the Basin made the same sound for a whole night: not static, but *singing*. Since then, people near Harrowgate sleep badly and dream the same dream. Some walk up the lift‑towers and don't come down. A prophet called **Last** preaches from the antenna farms: *the maelstrom is everyone who died; open your brain and they'll speak to you again.*

**What the Director knows** (this is a stakes question, not a fact):
- Last is the first person who ever woke from the stasis facility the Quarantine came out of, decades earlier.
- Last's Choir of brainers uses the golden‑age antenna farms to *broadcast into the maelstrom*.
- **Is Last right?** The game never answers. The endings let the player decide what's true by what they do.

**The World Clock.** A 6‑segment countdown, the same shape as a harm clock. It advances on Last's threat moves (about one segment every 6–10 months if nobody interferes) and on specific player failures. Player victories can push it back.

| Segment | Effect on the world |
|---|---|
| **3:00** | Everyone dreams the dream. *Open your brain* gets +1 everywhere, but a miss also gives 1 Ψ‑harm. Rumors of the Choir spread. |
| **6:00** | **Hum‑touched holds** appear: the *Delusion* affliction. Wisher falls. Hold Leans start drifting toward Last on their own. |
| **9:00** | The Choir **marches**: Last starts conquering holds like a warlord. Prophet threat moves happen twice a month. |
| **10:00** | **The Calm spreads.** In Hum zones, vehicles stall and radios sing. Travel through the Up is cut. |
| **11:00** | The antenna farm goes live. Hum zones join together. Every NPC with Weird+1 or more starts *listening*. |
| **12:00** | **Everybody hears it.** The campaign ends: an end state chosen by who you are and what you did (see [06](06-playbooks.md)). |

> **Why a clock and not a turn limit.** Inindo's tension came from Nobunaga's army *moving*, not from a timer. The World Clock only moves when Last acts and the player hasn't stopped it, so it is always the result of something that happened in the world, never arbitrary.

## The Golden Age (what's left)
Things the world still has, all scarce and all hinted at rather than explained:
- shipwrecks on dry salt
- container walls
- subway lines
- antenna farms
- the airship
- stasis facilities
- fused glass
- vault doors with dead keypads
- the lighthouses

Golden‑Age technology always comes with a **downside** for the Director to *activate* (an AW MC move).

## Naming rules
- **People** take AW‑style single names, nicknames and odd words: Tum Tum, Gnarly, Clarion, Dremmer, Omie Wise, Twice, Brace Win, Fianelly. Don't use real‑world full names. See [13](13-content-tables.md).
- **Places** are named for what they *do* or what they *are* now (Saltlick, Sweetwell, Burning Water), or for a person (Brace Win, Gnarly).
- **Nobody says "post‑apocalyptic".** People say "*the golden age*", "*before*" and "*the world*".
