# 02 · The World: the Quiet

## The premise
Nobody remembers how or why. The oldest people alive remember being children when the cities burned. Everyone remembers what came after: **the Howling**.

For a generation, the world's psychic maelstrom screamed across every open road. If you went more than a day out from a hold, you heard it. Then you stopped coming back, or you came back wrong. So people stayed put. Each hold knew its neighbours and nothing past them, and the great roads of the golden age, the interstates, lay empty for twenty‑five years.

Then, a little over a year ago, **it eased**. Nobody knows why. Out on the roads it's quiet now: not silent, but quiet enough. For the first time in a generation people are driving the interstates again, and nobody knows what's out there. Maps are twenty‑five years out of date. Whole holds have vanished, and others have grown up where nobody expected. Warlords who were only rumors have names and armies now. And somewhere at the edges the maelstrom still howls. People call those edges **the Loud**, and the part of the world that eased **the Quiet**.

> **Design notes.**
> - **Why the Easing.** It turns Apocalypse World into an **age of discovery**, the way UWNH's world was. Everything is unexplored *again*. Travel and exploration are the core of the game because they're *new* to everyone in it.
> - **The interstates are the seas.** UWNH's ships sailed oceans and couldn't cross land. Here, convoys drive the old highway network. The interstates are the open seas: wide, fast, and dangerous because they're exposed. Smaller highways are coastal waters. Off‑road is **the Rough**, the equivalent of UWNH's land. The seas are narrower, but they work the same way.
> - **Rivers cut both ways.** Some dry riverbeds are highways in their own right. Living rivers are barriers you can only cross at a few places, so those crossings become UWNH's straits: chokepoints that warlords fight over.
> - **Scale.** The map is a *portion* of the former US, Mexico and Canada: from the Lakes on the old Canadian border, south to the Gulf coast, and on into the Bravo and the Sierra Coast in the old Mexico. It's smaller than UWNH's world and dense with about **130 holds**.

## The map as a road network

| Feature | UWNH equivalent | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Interstate** ("the Ten", "the Thirty‑Five"…) | Open ocean | Fast, and wide enough for **road war** in 5 lanes. Exposed: you're seen from far off. Broken in places. |
| **Highway / state road** | Coastal waters | Slower (×0.7) and narrow (3 lanes), with more cover. Links holds off the interstates. |
| **Back road** | Shallows | Slow (×0.5). Only one rig wide. Big frames risk getting stuck (*deal with bad terrain*). |
| **Dry riverbed** (the Bravo, the Red, the Arkansas, the Pecos, the Brazos) | A sea lane nobody charted | Wide and flat. Fast for light rigs, and **sailable by land yachts**. Flash floods in storm season. |
| **The Rough** (off‑road) | Land | Bikes, buggies and half‑tracks only, at ×0.3 with *deal with bad terrain* every day. Haulers, buses and tankers can't go here at all. |
| **Living river** (the Mississippi, Ohio, Missouri, Tennessee; the Detroit and Niagara) | Land, except at straits | **Can't be crossed** except at a **crossing**: a bridge, a dam, a ferry, a tunnel, or a ford in a dry season. River water is poisoned. |
| **The Lakes** | A real sea | Open water. In summer you need ferries. **In winter they freeze, and ice roads open**: the fastest routes in the north for three months of the year. |
| **Break** (a collapsed overpass, a bridge out, a thousand‑car pileup) | Reefs and shoals | Blocks a lane or a whole road. Forces a detour through highways or the Rough. A Savvyhead project or a hardholder's labour can clear one. |
| **Interchange** (a stack, a cloverleaf, "the Spaghetti") | A strait or a busy harbour mouth | Where roads meet. Usually somebody's toll. |

**Survivors name the interstates by number** ("*running the Ten*", "*up the Fifty‑Five*", "*the Four‑Oh‑One's still froze*"). They talk about them the way sailors talked about seas.

## Old names
- Most holds are named for what they are now (*Saltlick*, *Burning Water*, *the Arch*) or for whoever runs them (*Gnarly*, *Partridge*). A few golden‑age names survive worn down (*Orleans*, *Tampico*, *Mobile*).
- **The Quarantine playbook knows the old names.** For them, the map shows golden‑age names under the new ones.
- The [Gazetteer](16-gazetteer.md) lists the real‑world site behind every hold, for designers.

## The world map: rings and directions
The overworld borrows the *shape* of AW's threat map, rings and eight directions, as its geography of danger. It's centred on **the Crossing**: the old Memphis bridges over the Mississippi, where the Lighthouse stands. The rings set event danger ([05](05-events.md#how-an-event-is-chosen)) and how much of the map starts fogged.

  WEST:                ┌───────────────────────────────┐            EAST:

  the Thirty‑Five      │  │ CLOSER: a week's drive  │   │   the Glass

**Rings**:

| Ring | What it is | Threat density | Fog |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centre** | The Crossing: the Memphis bridges and the junction of the Forty and the Fifty‑Five. Neutral by old custom. | Low | Always known |
| **Closer** | About a week's drive: the Delta, the Forty, the Twenty, the Sixty‑Five | Medium | Revealed by travel or bought charts |
| **Farther** | The far roads: the Ten, the Thirty‑Five, the Seventy, the Seventy‑Five, the Glass, the Rag‑Wastes, the Bravo, the Lakes, the Sierra Coast. Warlord seats, Up and Down entrances. | High, and rising with distance | Revealed by travel, rumors, the News |
| **Notional** | **The Loud** (the Out), the deep In, rumored places | Extreme | Not on the map until a rumor, a Sight or a discipline puts it there |

## Holds
Every hold has these:
- a **hardholder** (or a boss)
- a **Surplus** (what it sells cheap)
- a **Want** (what it pays dear for, and what goes wrong without it)
- **buildings**
- a **Lean**: who it's loyal to (see [17](17-factions.md) and [10](10-warlords-and-politics.md))

**About 70% of holds start independent.** Most of them haven't spoken to anyone outside their own valley in twenty‑five years. That leaves a lot of territory for the factions and the player to win over.

**Hold tiers**:

| Tier | Count | Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| **Seat** (faction) | 8 | Everything, plus the Seat. Can't be invested in. |
| **Major hold** | 38 | Market, bar, garage, infirmary, notice wall, plus 1–2 specials (congregation, brainer's room, exchange, wells, crossing toll) |
| **Hold** | 66 | Market, bar, notice wall, plus a garage *or* an infirmary |
| **Outpost** | 18 | One trader, one shelter, one rumor. A camera‑sized map. |

## Factions and sub‑factions
Politics has two layers.

**Factions** are six powerful **hardholders**, each sitting on something the map can't do without. They compete for the holds through **Lean**, each in a way that grows out of its geography. A seventh, **the Choir**, doesn't compete at all: it **silences** holds, and it's the Nobunaga of this world. Full write‑ups are in **[17 · Factions](17-factions.md)**.

| Faction | Hardholder | Seat | What it sits on | Wins holds by… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Lighthouse** | Clarion | The Lighthouse (the Crossing) | The central bridge over the Mississippi | Passage: free crossings |
| **Sweetwell** | Grome | Sweetwell (the Thirty‑Five) | The San Antonio aquifer | Thirst |
| **Highwater** | Abondo | Highwater (the Lakes) | The Great Lakes and the ice roads | Freight and winter |
| **The Exchange** | Rothschild | The Exchange (the Glass) | Florida's sealed peninsula of rich ruins | Debt and salvage |
| **Burning Water** | Parcher | Burning Water (the Ten) | The Texas City refineries: guzzoline | Fuel |
| **Cerro** | Otilia | Cerro (the Bravo) | Monterrey, a walled steel city in the mountains | Walls |
| **THE CHOIR** | **Last**, with Tum Tum | **Harrowgate** (Up) and **Pellet** (the Seventy) | The dead towers of Houston and the maelstrom itself | **Silence**: takes holds off the board |

**Sub‑factions** are the cults, road gangs, guilds and secret societies that cut across faction lines: the Keepers, the Teeth, the Family, the Tankermen and a dozen more. They never own holds, but they have chapters in them, they **back** factions, they supply most gigs and recruits, and the Choir can **turn** them. See **[18 · Sub‑factions](18-sub-factions.md)**.

Independent holds have their own hardholders, who act as minor warlords with a *Dictator* or *Family* temperament.

## Last and the Hum (the campaign antagonist)
**What everyone knows.**
- Around the time of the Easing, every radio on the Ten made the same sound for a whole night: not static, but *singing*.
- Since then, people along the Ten sleep badly and dream the same dream, and some walk into Harrowgate's lift‑towers and don't come down.
- A prophet called **Last** preaches from the space center's great dishes: *the maelstrom is everyone who died. We took it in so you could travel. When we let it out, they'll speak to you again.*

**What the writers know** (these are open questions, not facts):
- Last is the first person who ever woke from the stasis facility the Quarantine came out of, decades earlier.
- **Did the Choir cause the Easing?** The Choir's brainers have been drawing the maelstrom *into Harrowgate* through the dishes. The world is quiet because they're holding it.
- **What happens if they let go?** Nobody knows. The Choir says the dead would speak again. Everyone else fears the Howling coming back.
- **Is Last right?** The game never answers. The endings let the player decide what's true by what they do.

### The Choir's creep (no clock)
The Choir doesn't run on a timer. It spreads like rot, **one hold at a time, down the same roads you drive**, and the world gets harsher as it spreads. The rules are in [17](17-factions.md#the-choir-the-nobunaga-faction) and the numbers in [`choir.json`](../../data/rules/choir.json).
- **Hum level.** Every hold has a Hum level from 0 to 100. At **30** it's **Touched** (bad dreams, people leaving for Harrowgate). At **60** it's **Marked**: the Choir's circle appears on its doors, and its Lean starts draining to no one. At **100** it's **Silenced**, and it belongs to the Choir.
- **Seep.** Each month, Silenced holds seep Hum into the holds connected to them by road, weaker with distance. Deaf factions, Keepers, Watchers, your convoy patrolling and Static slow it. Turned sub‑factions speed it up.
- **Voices.** Every coffle Tum Tum's Catchers deliver, and every hold Silenced, adds to the Choir's **voices**, and more voices make the seep stronger.
- **Reach** is the number of Silenced holds. It sets how harsh the world is:

| Reach | Silenced holds | Effect on the world |
|---|---|---|
| **Whispers** | 0–4 | Everyone along the Ten dreams the dream. Rumors of the Choir spread. The Choir takes 1 action a month |
| **Spreading** | 5–14 | Howls (local flares of the old maelstrom) happen twice as often near Hum zones. Wisher falls. The Choir takes 2 actions a month. **The League can form** |
| **Marching** | 15–29 | The Opened **march**: the Choir attacks holds by gang war, like a warlord. The Arch starts singing, and the Hum spreads up the Seventy |
| **Drowning** | 30+ | **The Loud creeps in** at the edges of Hum zones, and travel through the Up is cut. Every NPC with Weird 50 or more starts *listening* |

- **Pace.** With no one resisting, the Choir Silences about 9 holds by month 36, 14 by month 60, 19 by month 72 and 42 by month 120 (the M1 simulation, 200 seeds: [report](../reports/m1-balance-report.md)). It's slow, and it's never hidden: the Month Report always says where it has spread.
- **It can be pushed back.** Stopping coffles, taking Pellet, Static, liberating Silenced holds, saving turned sub‑factions and breaking Harrowgate's nodes all lower Hum and voices.
- **There's no end state.** The Choir never ends the campaign. Your class's arc does, and the Choir's reach when it ends decides how dark the ending is ([06](06-playbooks.md#endings-and-the-choirs-reach)).

> **Why no clock.** Inindo's tension came from Nobunaga's army *moving*, not from a timer. Here the threat is a slow tide you can watch coming down the roads, and push back, at your own pace.

## Naming rules
- **People** take AW‑style single names, nicknames and odd words: Tum Tum, Gnarly, Clarion, Dremmer, Omie Wise, Brace Win, Fianelly. Along the Bravo and the Sierra Coast, names lean into Spanish words in the same spirit (Lupe, Rache, Otilia).
- **Places** are named for what they *do* or *are* now, for a person, or keep a worn golden‑age name. **Real geography is used as is**: real interstate numbers, rivers, lakes and the shape of the land. No hold is named after a brand.
- **Brands are set dressing, and rare.** After twenty‑five years of the Howling, most signage is rust and bare poles, and nobody cares what a logo meant. Occasionally, in a less‑rusted ruin, a sign survives. When it does, it's a **lookalike**: a plausible chain name you've never heard of, like *McCready's* or *Buckaroo's*, that you still recognise from its colours, mascot or sign shape. It's never a pun and never the real name or logo. See [13](13-content-tables.md#lookalike-brands-set-dressing) for rules and a seed list.
- **Spanish‑inflected speech** along the Bravo and the Sierra Coast. See [the voice guide below](#voice-the-bravo-and-the-sierra-coast).
- **Roads** are called by their number: "*the Ten*", "*the Four‑Oh‑One*".
- **Nobody says** "post‑apocalyptic", "America", "Mexico" or "Canada". People say "*the golden age*", "*before*", "*the Howling*", "*the Quiet*" and "*the world*".

## Voice: the Bravo and the Sierra Coast
Holds south of the Thirty‑Five and down the Mex 180 speak **Apocalypse World first, Spanish‑inflected second**. The voice is still AW: second person, profane, wry, scarcity and the maelstrom in every line. The inflection is in the rhythm and the vocabulary, not in turning the game into a different setting.

**Do:**
- Code‑switch the way border speech does: a Spanish word where it's the natural one. Terms of address (*mija*, *compa*, *jefe*), swearing (*pinche*, *cabrón*), small words (*pues*, *órale*, *ándale*, *ni modo*).
- Give the south its own names for the AW world in the same spirit: the maelstrom is *el remolino* or *la gritona* ("the screamer"); the Howling is *los años del grito*; the Easing is *la calma*; barter is *trueque*; jingle is *feria*; a rig is *la troca*; a hold is *el fuerte*.
- Keep at least 80% of every line in English. A player who reads no Spanish must never miss a choice, a price, a threat or a chance. **Ability names, UI text and choices are always in English.**

**Don't:**
- Write whole Spanish lines or paragraphs, or "translate" the AW voice into a folkloric or touristic one.
- Use stereotypes: no sombreros, siestas, cartel‑drama shorthand, or "magical" Mexico. The south is as broken, practical and weird as everywhere else.
- Let the inflection replace apocalyptica. Every southern scene still barfs forth the world: rust, thirst, the dishes humming on the horizon.

**Example**, at Falcon, from Rache, the dam's boss:
> "*Water's two feria a cask, compa, and that's me being nice. Before la calma nobody drove up here at all. Now everybody wants to drink my lake. Pues, ni modo. Pay or turn the troca around.*"

The Spanish here is the texture of the speech: the line carries the scarcity, the threat and the price, and an English‑only reader loses nothing.
