# 02 · The World: the Quiet

## The premise
Nobody remembers how or why. The oldest people alive remember being children when the cities burned. Everyone remembers what came after: **the Howling**.

For a generation, the world's psychic maelstrom screamed across every open road. If you went more than a day out from a hold, you heard it. Then you stopped coming back, or you came back wrong. So people stayed put. Each hold knew its neighbours and nothing past them, and the great roads of the golden age, the interstates, lay empty for twenty‑five years.

Then, a little over a year ago, **it eased**. Nobody knows why. Out on the roads it's quiet now: not silent, but quiet enough. For the first time in a generation people are driving the interstates again, and nobody knows what's out there. Maps are twenty‑five years out of date. Whole holds have vanished, and others have grown up where nobody expected. Warlords who were only rumors have names and armies now. And somewhere at the edges the maelstrom still howls. People call those edges **the Loud**, and the part of the world that eased **the Quiet**.

> **Design notes.**
> - **Why the Easing.** It turns Apocalypse World into an **age of discovery**, the way UWNH's world was. Everything is unexplored *again*. Travel and exploration are the core of the game because they're *new* to everyone in it.
> - **The interstates are the seas.** UWNH's ships sailed oceans and couldn't cross land. Here, convoys drive the old highway network. The interstates are the open seas: wide, fast, and dangerous because they're exposed. Smaller highways are coastal waters. Off‑road is **the Rough**, the equivalent of UWNH's land. The seas are narrower, but they work the same way.
> - **Rivers cut both ways.** Some dry riverbeds are highways in their own right. Living rivers are barriers you can only cross at a few places, so those crossings become UWNH's straits: chokepoints that warlords fight over.
> - **Scale.** The map is a *portion* of the former US, Mexico and Canada: from the Lakes on the old Canadian border, south to the Gulf coast, and on into the Bravo and the Sierra Coast in the old Mexico. It's smaller than UWNH's world and dense with about **130 holds**.

## The map as a road network

| Feature | UWNH equivalent | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Interstate** ("the Ten", "the Thirty‑Five"…) | Open ocean | Fast, and wide enough for **road war** in 5 lanes. Exposed: you're seen from far off. Broken in places. |
| **Highway / state road** | Coastal waters | Slower (×0.7) and narrow (3 lanes), with more cover. Links holds off the interstates. |
| **Back road** | Shallows | Slow (×0.5). Only one rig wide. Big frames risk getting stuck (*deal with bad terrain*). |
| **Dry riverbed** (the Bravo, the Red, the Arkansas, the Pecos, the Brazos) | A sea lane nobody charted | Wide and flat. Fast for light rigs, and **sailable by land yachts**. Flash floods in storm season. |
| **The Rough** (off‑road) | Land | Bikes, buggies and half‑tracks only, at ×0.3 with *deal with bad terrain* every day. Haulers, buses and tankers can't go here at all. |
| **Living river** (the Mississippi, Ohio, Missouri, Tennessee; the Detroit and Niagara) | Land, except at straits | **Can't be crossed** except at a **crossing**: a bridge, a dam, a ferry, a tunnel, or a ford in a dry season. River water is poisoned. |
| **The Lakes** | A real sea | Open water. In summer you need ferries. **In winter they freeze, and ice roads open**: the fastest routes in the north for three months of the year. |
| **Break** (a collapsed overpass, a bridge out, a thousand‑car pileup) | Reefs and shoals | Blocks a lane or a whole road. Forces a detour through highways or the Rough. A Savvyhead project or a hardholder's labour can clear one. |
| **Interchange** (a stack, a cloverleaf, "the Spaghetti") | A strait or a busy harbour mouth | Where roads meet. Usually somebody's toll. |

**Survivors name the interstates by number** ("*running the Ten*", "*up the Fifty‑Five*", "*the Four‑Oh‑One's still froze*"). They talk about them the way sailors talked about seas.

## Old names
- Most holds are named for what they are now (*Saltlick*, *Burning Water*, *the Arch*) or for whoever runs them (*Gnarly*, *Partridge*). A few golden‑age names survive worn down (*Orleans*, *Tampico*, *Mobile*).
- **The Quarantine playbook knows the old names.** For them, the map shows golden‑age names under the new ones.
- The [Gazetteer](16-gazetteer.md) lists the real‑world site behind every hold, for designers.

## The threat map *is* the world map
AW's threat map uses rings and eight directions. The overworld uses the same layout, centred on **the Crossing**: the old Memphis bridges over the Mississippi, where the Lighthouse stands. That means the map the Director reasons with and the map the player looks at are one object.

```
                       NORTH: the Seventy → the Lakes (old Canadian border)
           UP: Harrowgate                                  IN: the Maelstrom
           (the Houston skyline)                           (open your brain)
  WEST:                ┌───────────────────────────────┐            EAST:
  the Forty (west)     │ FARTHER: the far roads         │   the Sixty‑Five
  the Twenty           │  ┌─────────────────────────┐   │   the Seventy‑Five
  the Thirty‑Five      │  │ CLOSER: a week's drive  │   │   the Glass
  the Rag‑Wastes       │  │  ┌───────────────────┐  │   │
  the Bravo            │  │  │ CENTRE: Crossing  │  │   │
                       │  │  └───────────────────┘  │   │
                       │  └─────────────────────────┘   │
                       └───────────────────────────────┘
           DOWN: the Deep Lines                         OUT: the Loud
           (salt domes, caves, pipeline galleries)      (every road off the edge)
                       SOUTH: the Delta → the Ten → the Sierra Coast
```

**Rings**:

| Ring | What it is | Threat density | Fog |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centre** | The Crossing: the Memphis bridges and the junction of the Forty and the Fifty‑Five. Neutral by old custom. | Low | Always known |
| **Closer** | About a week's drive: the Delta, the Forty, the Twenty, the Sixty‑Five | Medium | Revealed by travel or bought charts |
| **Farther** | The far roads: the Ten, the Thirty‑Five, the Seventy, the Seventy‑Five, the Glass, the Rag‑Wastes, the Bravo, the Lakes, the Sierra Coast. Warlord seats, Up and Down entrances. | High, and rising with distance | Revealed by travel, rumors, the News |
| **Notional** | **The Loud** (the Out), the deep In, rumored places | Extreme | Not on the map until a rumor, sight or augury puts it there |

## Holds
Every hold has these:
- a **hardholder** (or a boss)
- a **Surplus** (what it sells cheap)
- a **Want** (what it pays dear for, and what goes wrong without it)
- **buildings**
- a **Lean**: who it's loyal to (see [10](10-warlords-and-politics.md))

**About three‑quarters of holds start independent.** Most of them haven't spoken to anyone outside their own valley in twenty‑five years. That leaves a lot of territory for warlords and the player to win over.

**Hold tiers**:

| Tier | Count | Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| **Seat** (warlord) | 7 | Everything, plus the Seat. Can't be invested in. |
| **Major hold** | 39 | Market, bar, garage, infirmary, notice wall, plus 1–2 specials (congregation, brainer's room, exchange, wells, crossing toll) |
| **Hold** | 66 | Market, bar, notice wall, plus a garage *or* an infirmary |
| **Outpost** | 18 | One trader, one shelter, one rumor. A camera‑sized map. |

Grome (the southwest's aquifer) and Abondo (the Lakes) are both Dictators who control water, at opposite ends of the map. Their rivalry, and the trade between them, is the long axis of the campaign. Independent holds have their own hardholders, who act as minor warlords using the *Dictator* or *Family* impulse.

## Last and the Hum (the campaign antagonist)
**What everyone knows.**
- Around the time of the Easing, every radio on the Ten made the same sound for a whole night: not static, but *singing*.
- Since then, people along the Ten sleep badly and dream the same dream, and some walk into Harrowgate's lift‑towers and don't come down.
- A prophet called **Last** preaches from the space center's great dishes: *the maelstrom is everyone who died. We took it in so you could travel. When we let it out, they'll speak to you again.*

**What the Director knows** (these are stakes questions, not facts):
- Last is the first person who ever woke from the stasis facility the Quarantine came out of, decades earlier.
- **Did the Choir cause the Easing?** The Choir's brainers have been drawing the maelstrom *into Harrowgate* through the dishes. The world is quiet because they're holding it.
- **What happens when they let go?** At 12:00 the Hum pours it all back out, through every opened brain at once.
- **Is Last right?** The game never answers. The endings let the player decide what's true by what they do.

**The World Clock is the end of the Easing.** It's a 6‑segment countdown, the same shape as a harm clock. It advances on Last's threat moves (about one segment every 8–12 months if nobody interferes) and on specific player failures. Player victories can push it back.

| Segment | Effect on the world |
|---|---|
| **3:00** | Everyone along the Ten dreams the dream. *Open your brain* gets +1 everywhere, but a miss also gives 1 Ψ‑harm. Rumors of the Choir spread. |
| **6:00** | **Hum‑touched holds** appear: the *Delusion* affliction. Wisher falls. Leans along the Ten drift toward Last on their own. **Howls** (local flares of the old maelstrom) happen twice as often. |
| **9:00** | The Choir **marches**: Last starts conquering holds like a warlord. Prophet threat moves happen twice a month. The Arch starts singing, and the Hum spreads up the Seventy. |
| **10:00** | **The Loud creeps in.** The edge holds go silent, one ring of the map turns Notional again, and travel through the Up is cut. |
| **11:00** | The dishes go live. Hum zones join along every interstate. Every NPC with Weird+1 or more starts *listening*. |
| **12:00** | **Everybody hears it.** The Easing ends. The campaign ends: an end state chosen by who you are and what you did (see [06](06-playbooks.md)). |

> **Why a clock and not a turn limit.** Inindo's tension came from Nobunaga's army *moving*, not from a timer. Here the window of the Easing is closing, but only because Last is closing it. You can push it back.

## Naming rules
- **People** take AW‑style single names, nicknames and odd words: Tum Tum, Gnarly, Clarion, Dremmer, Omie Wise, Brace Win, Fianelly. Along the Bravo and the Sierra Coast, names lean into Spanish words in the same spirit (Lupe, Rache, Otilia).
- **Places** are named for what they *do* or *are* now, for a person, or keep a worn golden‑age name. No hold is named after a real company, brand or trademarked attraction.
- **Roads** are called by their number: "*the Ten*", "*the Four‑Oh‑One*".
- **Nobody says** "post‑apocalyptic", "America", "Mexico" or "Canada". People say "*the golden age*", "*before*", "*the Howling*", "*the Quiet*" and "*the world*".
