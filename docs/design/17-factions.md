# 17 · Factions

> In UWNH, Portugal, Spain, England, Holland, Italy and the Ottomans fought over the ports with **investment**, each nation's share of each port rising and falling. In Inindo, the daimyo fought over provinces, and over all of them loomed **Nobunaga**, who didn't play by the same rules.
>
> Dark Horizon has both. **Six influence factions** compete for the 130 holds through **Lean**, each with its own way of winning loyalty. And **the Choir** doesn't compete for Lean at all. It **silences** holds, and it's the most dangerous thing on the map.

## The six influence factions

Every faction plays the same **Lean** game (see [07](07-economy.md#investment-and-lean-uwnhs-allegiance-aws-way)), but each one has:
- an **influence engine**: how *it* gains Lean in the world turn, and what makes it different to fight
- a **patron package**: what you get if you swear to it, as UWNH's nation choice shaped your career
- a **demand**: what it asks of its patrons
- a **weakness**: how a rival, or you, takes holds away from it

### The Keepers: *"Keep the light on."*
**Who they are.** When the Howling started, someone on the Memphis bridge refused to let the lights go out. Through twenty‑five years of nobody travelling, the Keepers kept lamps lit on bridges and towers, so that *if* anyone ever came, they'd find their way. They were a joke for a generation. Since the Easing, they're the only people with any idea of the roads.
- **Leader**: **Kindle**, an old woman who has never left the bridge and knows every crossing by its sound.
- **Flavour**: lanterns hung from every rig, hospitality as a sacrament, crossings kept free on principle. Their creed is half religion and half traffic control. They're the closest thing to *good* on the map, and they're unbearably stubborn.
- **AW kind**: a Cult (Brute), with the impulse *to victimize & incorporate people* bent toward *to take everyone in*. It's benign, but it's still a cult.
- **Influence engine: Light.** A Keeper lamp in a hold (put up as a gig or by a Keeper caravan) gives slow Lean that is **sticky**: it resists other factions' drift by 50%. Keepers gain extra Lean from **first contacts** and **charts** sold to them.
- **Patron package**:
  - **The lamp mark**: free passage at Keeper crossings, and shelter at any Keeper hold.
  - Charts sell to Keepers at +50%.
  - Exploration gigs.
- **Demand**: never charge a toll at a crossing you hold, and bring every first contact back to the Lighthouse.
- **Weakness**: they won't fight first. Their gangs only *defend something they hold*. Anyone willing to take a bridge by force can.
- **Starting holds**: the Lighthouse (seat), Helena, Fork, Weigh Station.
- **Natural fit**: the **Driver**. The Keepers' arc and the Driver's arc meet at Last Stop.

### The Exchange: *"Everything has a price, and we wrote it down."*
**Who they are.** Rothschild opened the only working vault in the Glass during the Howling and started lending against what was inside. Now half the holds on the map owe the Exchange something.
- **Leader**: **Rothschild**, polite and patient. Collects golden‑age relics and **charts**. Never raises a voice and never forgets a debt.
- **Flavour**: ledgers, stamped notes, gold teeth, clerks with shotguns, holes punched in old coins. The Exchange's gangs are well‑armed and expensive.
- **Influence engine: Money.** It **invests** in holds (as you do in UWNH) and **lends**. Every indebted hold slides 2 Lean a month toward the Exchange until the debt is paid.
- **Patron package**:
  - **Letter of credit**: borrow at 5% instead of 10%.
  - Exclusive‑chart buyer at triple price.
  - Relic‑hunting gigs.
- **Demand**: sell relics to them first, and collect debts for them.
- **Weakness**: money doesn't fight. A hold the Exchange owns but can't protect is ripe for the Teeth. Paying off a hold's debt to the Exchange *breaks* the slide.
- **Starting holds**: the Exchange (seat), Mirror Town, the Kingdom, Hill, Fuse.
- **Natural fit**: the **Hardholder** and the **Savvyhead**.

### The Lake Compact: *"Ice in winter, iron in summer, and nothing moves without us."*
**Who they are.** The Lakes kept their water through the Howling, and Highwater kept its fleet of ice skiffs and lake boats. The Compact is a league of northern holds bound by freight contracts, run from Toronto by a man who prefers terms to wars.
- **Leader**: **Abondo**, calm and courteous, and absolutely in control of the north's water and roads.
- **Flavour**: wool, steel, frost, ledgers of freight; disciplined **Lakemen** escorts on the ice roads; rules for everything.
- **Influence engine: Freight.** Every hold on a **standing Compact caravan route** gains Lean each month. **In winter**, when the ice roads open, that gain doubles and the Compact pushes south down the Seventy with tankers of fresh water.
- **Patron package**:
  - **The ice‑road pass**: winter crossings of the Lakes.
  - Freight contracts (steady delivery gigs).
  - Water bought cheaply in the north.
- **Demand**: carry Compact freight first, and never undercut Compact water prices.
- **Weakness**: a broken route loses its whole chain. Raiding one caravan costs the Compact Lean all along the route, and spring thaw closes the ice roads.
- **Starting holds**: Highwater (seat), the Falls, the Tunnel, Pen, Forks.
- **Rivals**: Sweetwell. The water trade between north and south is the long axis of the map.

### Sweetwell: *"You'll drink when I say."*
**Who they are.** Grome owns the springs under the old city of San Antonio, the only reliable water in the southwest. Sweetwell's power is simple: everyone downstream is thirsty.
- **Leader**: **Grome**, rigid and righteous. Thinks of the water ration as justice.
- **Flavour**: hazmat blue, numbered water chits, the **Wellguard** in sealed helmets, tanker convoys under guard, public rationing.
- **Influence engine: Thirst.** Any hold with a **water want of 2 or more** within tanker range drifts toward Sweetwell each month. **The Long Heat** doubles the drift. Grome can also *shut off* a hold, which creates a water‑want affliction.
- **Patron package**:
  - **Water chits**: cheap water anywhere in the southwest.
  - Tanker escort gigs.
  - The Wellguard leave you alone.
- **Demand**: obedience, enforcing rations, and never selling Compact water in the south.
- **Weakness**: give a hold its own water (fixing the Pumphouse, Falcon's dam, fog nets, a Savvyhead condenser) and Sweetwell's hold on it ends at once.
- **Starting holds**: Sweetwell (seat), Mission, Blue Hole, Burning Water.

### The Teeth: *"Pay, or we eat."*
**Who they are.** The biggest raider pack in the southwest, a moving city of bikes and war rigs that has followed Dremmer since the Howling made the open roads empty and therefore *his*.
- **Leader**: **Dremmer**, the Alpha wolf. Brave, cruel and generous to his own.
- **Flavour**: bone, chrome, engine roar, trophies wired to grilles. The camp moves every season.
- **Influence engine: Fear.** Raids and tribute give Lean **fast**: up to +15 a month in a single hold. But fear **decays** by 5 a month once the Teeth aren't nearby. Their territory is wide and brittle.
- **Patron package**:
  - **The tooth mark**: raiders leave your convoy alone in the west.
  - A share of raid spoils.
  - Recruits for a gang.
- **Demand**: ride when called, and pay a tithe of your take.
- **Weakness**: defend one hold against one raid, publicly, and fear collapses in all the holds nearby. Kill a lieutenant and that lieutenant's holds are contested at once.
- **Starting holds**: the Teeth (seat), Twice, Nodders, Drift, Dog Head, Rumhouse, the Stack, Brand, Gullet, Derricks, Stockade.
- **Natural fit**: the **Chopper**.

### The Family: *"There's always room at the table."*
**Who they are.** Under the salt domes and pipeline tunnels, Mother Krin took in everyone the Howling broke. The Family is enormous, mostly unseen, and everywhere.
- **Leader**: **Mother Krin**, the Hive queen. Warm, terrifying, hungry.
- **Flavour**: lamplight in tunnels, mushroom farms, shared bowls, too many cousins. Every Family member calls every other one by a kin‑word.
- **Influence engine: Kin.** The Family plants **cousins** in holds. Their Lean is **hidden** on the map until revealed by *read a sitch*, a spy gig, or a brainer. A hold can be 40% Family without anyone knowing, until one night the gates open from the inside. **This is Inindo's ninja clans: spies, infiltration, betrayal.**
- **Patron package**:
  - **The tunnel key**: Down travel and shortcuts under the rivers.
  - Spy and infiltration gigs.
  - Intelligence: hidden Lean revealed anywhere they have cousins.
- **Demand**: "*Bring us your strays*": recruits, orphans, the desperate. And meat.
- **Weakness**: exposure. Reveal the cousins in a hold publicly and the Family's Lean there drops to 0.
- **Starting holds**: Krin's Nest (seat), the Reserve. The real number is hidden.
- **Danger**: **Hum‑touched.** If the World Clock reaches 9:00 and the Family hasn't been turned away from Last, **the Family falls to the Choir**, and every hidden cousin becomes a door for it.

## THE CHOIR: the Nobunaga faction

> *"We took the screaming into us so you could walk the roads. You're welcome. Now come and listen."*

**Who they are.** In the dead towers of Harrowgate and under the space center's dishes, the prophet **Last** and a choir of brainers say they swallowed the maelstrom and caused the Easing. Maybe they did. They hold it in Harrowgate. It's getting loud in there, and when they let it go (12:00 on the [World Clock](02-world.md#last-and-the-hum-the-campaign-antagonist)), everyone will hear it at once.
- **Leader**: **Last**, first out of the stasis facility decades ago. Calm, sorrowful, utterly certain.
- **The flesh‑trade arm**: **Tum Tum**, the Slaver of Pellet, sells the Choir bodies. His Catchers run coffles down the Fifty‑Five and the Ten to Harrowgate. Tum Tum is the Choir's *ordinary* face: a slaver you can trade with, shoot at and influence. The Choir behind him is not ordinary at all.
- **The cast**:
  - **the Opened**: people whose brains the Choir has opened. They walk, fight and work, and they don't speak in their own voices any more.
  - **brainer cantors**: Grotesque / Mindfucker. **Sings‑Back** is the worst of them.
  - **Tum Tum's Catchers**: Brute / Hunting pack.
- **Look**: the only faction drawn in **red**, which the art direction reserves for blood, harm and the Hum. A red circle painted on doors. Radios that sing. Rows of the Opened standing very still on overpasses, all facing Harrowgate.

### How the Choir breaks the rules
Every other faction plays the influence game. The Choir is built to be **distinctly more dangerous**, in the way Nobunaga was in Inindo, and players should feel the difference from the first time they meet it.

| Rule everyone else follows | What the Choir does instead |
|---|---|
| Factions compete for **Lean**, and holds can be bought back | The Choir **Silences** a hold. A Silenced hold is **off the influence board**: no Lean, no market, no recruiting, no gigs. Its people are Opened. **You can't buy it back.** It can only be **liberated**: a gang war at the gates, then a counter (the Static discipline, a Hocus *Frenzy*, an augury *contain*, or the Savvyhead's jammer) to close the people's brains again. |
| Gangs break when their leader falls, and flee or surrender under harm ([09](09-combat.md#harm-reference-from-aw)) | **The Opened never break.** They have no cohesion threshold, can't be *go aggro*'d, and ignore *impress, dismay or frighten*. They fight until destroyed. They're unarmored and half‑starved, so they're easy to hurt, but they never stop. |
| Battle harm is physical | **Cantors deal Ψ‑harm at range**, ignoring armor. Any crew member with Hum exposure 3 or more rolls +Hx with you each round to resist **turning mid‑fight**. |
| Warlords get 1–3 actions a month by grip | The Choir gets **2 actions a month from 0:00, 3 from 6:00, and marches (gang war conquest) every month from 9:00.** |
| You can outrun a warlord | You can't outrun the Choir. It reaches you through **Hum exposure**: in dreams, through crew who repeat its words, and in the In. |
| Warlords negotiate, offer marks and post gigs | **The Choir doesn't deal.** There's no mark, no patronage and no gig. Only Tum Tum trades (in people, which the player can never buy or sell). Joining the Choir isn't patronage. It's an **ending** ("*Join them*"). |
| Factions are rivals to each other | The Choir **absorbs** factions. Hum‑touched factions fall to it (the Family at 9:00 unless prevented). Any faction whose seat is Silenced is destroyed, and its holds become contested. |
| Holds change hands one at a time | Silenced holds turn their roads into **Hum zones**: permanent Howls on nearby interstates, trade routes cut, and the Loud creeping in from 10:00. |
| A warlord's power is its gangs | The Choir's power is **people**. Every Silenced hold and every coffle that reaches Harrowgate feeds it. |

### What makes it beatable (fan rules still apply)
The Choir is more dangerous, not unfair. The Director still *announces future badness* before it hits:
- The Month Report always prints where the Choir moved.
- The red circle shows up on a hold's doors a month before that hold is Silenced.

The ways to hit back:
- **Cut the supply line.** Attack Tum Tum's coffles, free the Catchers' captives, and take Pellet. Every coffle stopped slows the World Clock. Taking Pellet pushes it back one segment.
- **Liberate Silenced holds.** It's slow and costly, but each liberation pushes the clock back and the freed hold leans hard toward whoever freed it.
- **Protect the vulnerable.** Turn the Family away from Last before 9:00 (a Family arc of gigs, *seduce or manipulate* on Mother Krin, or exposing the Choir's cousins inside the Family).
- **Build the League** (below).
- **Strike Harrowgate**: each playbook's endgame (see [06](06-playbooks.md)).

### The League (Inindo's anti‑Nobunaga coalition)
History gave Nobunaga a league of enemies. Here, from **6:00** the influence factions can be brought into **the League** against the Choir. The player is the natural broker, and only a broker who has standing with several factions can hold it together.
- **Forming it**: each faction joins when the player brings it a reason. The engine treats that as a *seduce or manipulate* on the faction leader, with the reason supplied by gigs done, Lean shared, debts forgiven, or a Silenced hold liberated in front of them.
- **What it does**: League factions stop fighting each other (no conquest between members), share gang strength for liberation wars, and march on Harrowgate together at the end.
- **Why it's hard**: the members still want each other's holds. The League has its own **countdown**, which ticks on every inter‑faction grievance (a raid, a debt called in, water shut off). At 12:00 it collapses.
- **Natural tensions**:
  - Sweetwell and the Lake Compact (water)
  - the Teeth and everyone (fear)
  - the Exchange and its debtors
  - the Keepers and anyone who charges tolls
