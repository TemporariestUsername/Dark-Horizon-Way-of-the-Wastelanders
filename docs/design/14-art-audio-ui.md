# 14 · Art, Audio and UI

## Visual identity: "the playbook, brought to life"
The AW playbooks look like **stark white silhouettes cut out of black**, with ragged torn edges, **distressed stencil type**, and paper sheets marked with ink. Inindo's title screen uses the same grammar, with a **blood‑red** banner as its only colour.

**Rules:**
1. **Two tones and one red.** All gameplay art is drawn in **bone white** (#EDE6D6) and **char black** (#0E0D0C), with dithered greys (a 4‑step ordered dither). **Red** (#B3201B) is reserved for **blood, harm, the Hum, and countdown fill**. Nothing else is red. **The Choir is the only faction drawn in red**: its sigil (a red circle), its doors and its banners. That way the player learns to read the colour itself as danger.
2. **Silhouette first.** Characters, rigs and threats have to read as solid shapes at 1× scale. Portraits are AW‑style cut‑outs: a high‑contrast face, a black field and ragged edges.
3. **Paper UI.** Menus are the **playbook sheet**: off‑white paper, black ink, stencil headings with torn rules (`▬▬▬▬`), and circle checkboxes for moves and highlights.
4. **The In is the negative.** The In layer inverts the palette: white ground, black sky, red veins.
5. **Scale.** Native 480×270 pixel art with 16×16 tiles. Portraits are 64×80.

## Signage and ruins
- **Most signage is gone.** It's rust, bare poles and sun‑bleached blanks. Interstate shields and mile markers survive best, because they're metal and they matter; they're drawn crisp because people still use them.
- **Lookalike brands** appear rarely, in less‑rusted ruins only, as faded or half‑lit signs drawn in the same two tones (red only if the sign is lit by a hold's power). They should be recognisable from silhouette and palette alone: the art carries the reference, not the name. Rules and seed list: [13](13-content-tables.md#lookalike-brands-set-dressing).

## Faction marks
Each faction has a **stencil sigil** in bone and black, painted on its holds' gates, its rigs and its gangs' gear. On the map, a hold's Lean is drawn as a torn‑paper bar split by those sigils.

| Faction | Sigil | Look |
|---|---|---|
| The Lighthouse | A lamp with three rays | White armbands, lamps on everything, long tables |
| Sweetwell | A drop inside a ring | Sealed helmets, numbered chits, tanker convoys |
| Highwater | Three wave lines | Wool, frost, matching coats, ice skiffs |
| The Exchange | A coin with a hole punched through | White suits going green, gold teeth, ledgers |
| Burning Water | A flare stack | Soot, welding masks, scorched tankers, the flare on the horizon |
| Cerro | A mountain saddle over a bar of steel | Cement grey, rebar crowns, slag walls, plated war rigs |
| **The Choir** | **A red circle** | **The only red banners on the map.** The Opened standing still on overpasses, all facing Harrowgate |

**Sub‑faction marks** ([18](18-sub-factions.md)) are smaller, scrawled and personal. They're never painted on gates, only tagged, worn or hidden:
- the Keepers' lantern hung at a crossing
- the Teeth's jaw scratched into a road sign
- the Wheelwrights' cog stamped on a repaired part
- the Tankermen's union patch
- the Players' chalked note on a wall
- the Family's open hand, drawn only where kin will see it
- the Odd Lot's knot of wire on a fence post

Spotting a hidden sub‑faction mark is a *read a sitch* result.

## Iconography
- **Harm clock**: a 6‑segment pie (3, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12), filled in red ink. The same shape is used for **all countdowns** and the **World Clock**, so the player learns to read one symbol.
- **Hx**: a signed number in a hand‑drawn box.
- **Highlights**: a red circle around a stat.
- **Lean**: a torn‑paper bar split by faction stencil marks.

## Typography
- **Display**: a distressed stencil sans in the style of AW's headers (licensed or commissioned).
- **Body**: a clean book serif. Moves are set in **bold italic**, as AW sets them.
- **Director text**: an italic serif.

## Audio
- **Palette**: drones, rusted percussion (oil drums, chains), detuned radio, a lone baritone guitar, wind on salt.
- **The Hum** is the game's leitmotif. It's a low choral tone **mixed under every track, getting louder by World Clock segment**. At 3:00 it's barely audible. At 11:00 it's in everything. At 12:00 it *resolves* into a chord.
- **Diegetic radio**: rigs have radios. Tuning in finds the News, Rumhouse music, and later the Choir.
- **Dice**: bone dice on paper. The 10+ / 7–9 / miss results each have their own sting.
- **Silence** is a tool. The game opens almost silent (it's the Quiet). A Howl brings back the old roar for a few seconds, then cuts to nothing but wind and radio hiss.

## Accessibility
- Full dice transparency (every modifier is named).
- Harm and countdowns are readable without colour, by segment count.
- Text speed and an instant‑text option. Large‑text mode for the paper UI.
- The Intimacy content toggle (see [00](00-vision.md#tone-and-content-policy)). Gore‑level toggle.
- **Mouse and keyboard first** (Windows and macOS), with remappable keys. Gamepad supported through Godot's focus system. Full trackpad support on Macs.
- Scalable UI for laptop screens down to 1280×800, including Retina scaling.
